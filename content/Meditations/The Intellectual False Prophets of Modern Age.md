About a century after the conquest of the northern kingdom of Israel by Assyrians ( ~ 720s BCE ), the Babylonians came to prominence dominating the ancient Near East, while the Assyrians were getting weaker by day. 

It was at the time of the REAL and original reformer ( unlike Luther,  joking LOL  ) Josiah, that the prophet Jeremiah started his prophetic service. After Josiah was killed by the Egyptians, they put his son Johoahaz on throne, whom they quickly deposed and installed another son of Josiah, Johoakim, as a puppet pro-Egypt king. Around 605 BCE, the Babylonian prince Nebuchadnezzar II defeats the Egyptian army and becomes the master of the entire region. He eventually marched to Jerusalem and  Johoaikim entered vassalage. A couple of years later,  the Judean king grows belligerent and stops paying tribute to the Babylonians.  It was at this moment that the prophet Jeremiah uttered the prophesy

> **13** "For from the least to the greatest of them, everyone is greedy for unjust gain; and from prophet to priest, everyone deals falsely.  
> **14** They have treated the wound of my people carelessly, saying, 'Peace, peace,' when there is no peace."  **Jeremiah 6:13-14 (NRSV)**

He told this prophesy amidst the crowd of prophets hyping up the king to continue rebelling against the Babylonians and side with the Egyptians. Jeremiah warned everybody that unless the King voluntarily submits to the encroaching Babylonian power, destruction of the city and of the temple was immanent. But the King did not listen. And the Babylonians besieged the city, killed the king's family, deported the aristocrats to Babylon along with the King and destroyed the temple.

### Christian Apologetics

'Apologetics' —a word that has roots in the Greek word $ἀπολογία$ (apologia) along with words like 'Apology' and 'Apologize'— is , in its dictionary definition,  'systematic argumentative discourse' (Mariam Webster) in defense of a claim. So by "Christian Apologetics", we are referring to a systematic argumentative discourse with which one defends the Christian claims. This endeavor has been practiced by Christians for as long as Christianity existed. We can, for example, consider the Gospel of Matthew to be an apologetic work in defense of the messiah-ship of Jesus Christ. Notable among the early apologists of Christianiy, is the church father Justine Martyr, who was active around the middle of the 2nd century CE. He apologized ( ie defended ) against the claims of the pagans, who said that Christianity is irrational. These days, apologetics has grown so much that it is nowadays given as a standalone degree program in many theological schools.

While I do enjoy the works of early and medieval Christian apologists, I take issue with the modern ones in many aspects. Not only that, I see  a staggering resemblance between contemporary apologists and the false prophets in the book of Jeremiah. 

### The Prophesy 
Picture this (to be quite honest with you, you don't have to go so far as picturing it. its a lived experience of every last Christian on earth), you are a Christian, and that you are having hard time making sense of many of the teachings of Christianity and  of the stories in the Bible. What even is the Bible? Who wrote it? Whats up with all these gruesome commands that God is presumably giving to Israelites to go kill an entire nation, or with those permissions allowing them to enslave people? Why is there so many versions of Christianity, each claiming to be the true Christians and denigrating the others? Not only that, you are also doubting if you can really for sure say God exists, much less the Christian God. You are not sure if what you believe to be true is really true and whether or not other religions are really false. 

In the middle of your faith crisis, you meet an online apologist, who goes by the name Blank Turek, and he tells you that he has all the answers for the questions that have been troubling your mind all this time. He tells you that the Bible is the word of God and that all the supposed problematic stories you read in the Bible, when read in "context", are not really problematic. The version of Christianity you are following, which is what the apologist himself follows, is the true and most rational version of Christianity.   Of God's existence, he tells you, there is nothing to worry about. Because if there is no God, there is no morality, right or wrong, good or bad. He says "Everything about our faith is rational and logically defensible" and that you shouldn't really be worried at all.  And after listening to his answers, you once again regain your confidence. "Who is this prophet who promised to answer to all my doubts and delivered, true to his words?", you say with a smile on your face and a big heartwarming faith circulating your veins. 

### Behold Babylon 
Just like how the Babylonians came crashing the city and giving reality-check to the King and his ally prophets, soon after you start getting deep into the issues related to your questions ( may be you don't like peace, or maybe you are genuinely curious )  that Turek has supposedly answered successfully, you realize much of what his said is either fallacious or outright lie. This sounds oddly specific scenario to imagine, and rightfully so. Because this is the reality of myself and many fellow Christians who went down this path. 

Contemporary apologists promise you that they can give a reasonable justification for the belief in the inerrancy and infallibility of Scriptures. You take their words and try to look into it yourself, only to be disappointed by the reality of the nature of the Bible, its composition and history. You soon realize that, far from being inerrant, the Bible is full of competing theological and ideological agendas, morally problematic teachings and stories, and of many historically inaccurate narratives . Seen in its own context, the Bible is more problematic than how you once thought.   You also learn that proving that your God, the God of Christianity, exists is not as easy as the apologist made it out to be. And frequently, the rationales he gave you mostly are rhetorical sleights of hand. 

Apologists aren't healing you from the wound that you have developed through your doubts. They are only giving you band-aids, to help you cope-up with it. Sooner or later, you discover the reality; You will see that the wound you thought healed was just lurking under the band waiting to be poked by a serious study of the Bible and proper introduction to philosophy. From here on, you are on your own. There is nobody coming to save you. And that is exactly how many people have lost and are losing their Christian faith all-together. 

I don't want to be naive and assert that all people who went through doubts  will eventually discover the reality. Many Christians who were doubting their faith at some point, will discover the apologetic works and continue to take the placebo, and more often than not joining the very endeavor to proselytize and "help" others who are similarly struggling with their faith.

I also don't want this to be taken as talking about any and all modern apologists. This is merely my observation of the trend contemporary, specially popular, apologetics is heading with. 


To put this concisely, apologists are the  modern versions of the false prophets in the Book of Jeremiah, in that they promise you full-fledged justification and rationale, that won't hold up to scrutiny when faced with reality.
