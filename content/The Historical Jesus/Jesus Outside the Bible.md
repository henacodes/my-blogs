If He is highly popular among the people who don't believe in him when he is not physically present, how popular would he had been when he was physically present on the face of the earth? Right ?

Well No! Jesus for most part of his life lived and walked around the region of Galilee, a place in ancient Israel where most people lived low-economy life. He was preaching around this low-profile area. It wasn't until the year 39 AD that Galilee become Roman province, after being ruled by Herod the Great and his sons for decades. And if a region is not considered a province, then the romans couldn't care less about it as long as the governor paid taxation. They will only start interacting when an outsider tries to invade it or when political instability occurs in the region. So this leads us to my first point. Jesus made little to no influence on the outside world ( non-Galilean world ) for the most part of his life, because He spent most of his life in a little rural region.

It was after his move to Judea that things started changing. Unlike their Jewish brothers at Galilee, Judeans were under governorship of a rome-appointed ruler, meaning Judea was already province by the time Jesus was preaching. And Jesus preaching at the Temple,which is at Jerusalem Judea , got him spottedin the eyes of Romans, culminating in his death by crucifixion. Now, i think, this is the time where his 2 - 3 years of service would have been introduced to the wider outer world. Even yet, it needed decades of spread of Christianity that was influential enough to get us couple of documents that mention this Jewish itinerant who walked around the shores Galilee. I am mentioning this so as to help you lower your expectation regarding Jesus in non-affiliated sources. So now we shall discuss a couple of non-biblical sources from antiquity where we have some information regarding Jesus' life.

## Pliny the Younger

Pliny was a roman governor of Bithynia and Pontus ( present day Turkey ). In his letter to his emperor Trajan , by the time, 112 AD, he asks the emperor for advice on what to do with Christian in his province. [1]

> … on a stated day they had been accustomed to meet before daybreak and to recite a hymn among themselves to Christ, as though he were a god, and that so far from binding themselves by oath to commit any crime, their oath was to abstain from theft, robbery, adultery, and from breach of faith …

Eventhough, the subject of discussion are Christians, Pliny indirectly mentions that Jesus was being worshipped by Christians as though he were god. This gives us the idea that Jesus was being exalted as God as far back as first century.

## Tacitus

Tacitus,one of the famous Roman Historians , records something thats related to Christians, thereby mentioning Jesus along the way, just like Pliny. In his famous work “Annals“ [2] , He says

> Chrestus, the founder of the name, had undergone the death penalty in the reign of Tiberius, by sentence of the procurator Pontius Pilatus,​

Here Tacitus was saying how Nero accused Christians of the fire he himself started and along the way he explains where the name Christian came from. Thats where the above quote was taken from. Contrary to Pliny , we can see that Tacitus has a bit more information about Jesus himself, which is that Jesus was sentenced a death penalty by the Roman governor Pontius Pilate which is consistent with what we find in our canonical gospels.

## Flavius Josephus

This guy, Josephus, is our primary source where have a detailed description of first century Israel and Judaism. If you have ever been in touch with modern christian apologetics even for a bit you would robably know this guy , atleast to some extent. He is a Jew who used to be commander of Jewish troops defending the Temple at Jerusalem , only to be defeated by the romans and serve the emperor as a historian from then on. We have two refernces regarding Jesus in his writing called “ Antiquities of Jews”.

The first , rather not so popular, mention comes from chapter 20 of his work and says

> So he assembled the sanhedrim of judges, and brought before them the brother of Jesus who was called Christ, whose name was James: and some others; [3]

Josephus here was narrating about how abusive the high priest Ananus was, giving James, the brother of Jesus as an example of victim. This story seems to perfectly align with what we know of James in our new testament. So its clear that Jesus indeed had a brother named James , who was later executed .

The 2nd and most controversial work from antiquity that mentions jesus is the very work Josephus wrote.

> Now there was about this time Jesus, a wise man;…. For he was a doer of wonderful works; a teacher of such men as receive the truth with pleasure. … He was [the] Christ. And when Pilate, at the suggestion of the principal men among us, had condemned him to the cross;[7](https://penelope.uchicago.edu/josephus/ant-18.html#margin_note_7) those that loved him at the first did not forsake him. For he appeared to them alive again, the third day:[8](https://penelope.uchicago.edu/josephus/ant-18.html#margin_note_8) as the divine prophets had foretold these and ten thousand other wonderful things concerning him. And the tribe of Christians, so named from him, are not extinct at this day. [ 4]

This is by far the most and elaborate mention of Jesus from what we have looked so far. Here Josephus is outright saying that Jesus was the Messiah and was crucified and rose from dead on the third day. And thats bizarre knowing full fact that Josephus was a Jew who didnt adhere to any form of Christianity. So whats the deal here ? I won't go into the weeds regarding the scholarship around this passage, but the majority of scholars, as far as my knowledge goes, subscribe to the idea that there was initially an original authentic mention of Jesus. But later Christians who copied works of Josephus didn't hesitate to interpolate the passage to mske it rhyme with Christian Dogma.
